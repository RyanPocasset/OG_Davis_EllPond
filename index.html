Long Pond & AWC Tree Models

34 GPS trees (4, 9 and 18 Nov 2026), numbered by waypoint time and matched to crowns in the USGS Rhode Island lidar (29 Mar 2022, leaf-off), over RIDOT leaf-off orthophotos.

How these numbers were made

Heights are metres above a ground model built from the lidar's ground returns. Crowns come from a 0.5 m canopy height model split into individual trees (treetop detection plus watershed). Cedars (AWC/WC) use a narrow-crown setting, because their 3–5 m spires merge under the standard one.

Because the flight was leaf-off, evergreen crowns return strong, dense first returns and bare hardwood crowns return weak ones. Each crown is called evergreen-like (upper-crown intensity ≥ 750), leaf-off hardwood (≤ 550) or ambiguous. Those cut-offs come from the dip in the intensity histogram of ~1,700 crowns, set without the field species labels.

Waypoints were averaged on a receiver good to about ±1.8 m, so each stem should sit under its own crown. GPS fixes are first shifted 1.28 m from the GPS's ITRF frame into the lidar's NAD83(2011). A tree then takes the nearest type-compatible crown whose outline is within 3 m of the stem, one crown per tree; a conifer can't take a bare-hardwood crown. High: stem inside the crown and the type fits. Medium: inside with an ambiguous type or no code, or within 2 m with the type fitting. Low: farther out, or flagged: the stem sits under another tree's crown, the treetop lies beyond the crown radius, or a conifer's canopy over the stem is more than 3 m shorter than the crown. None: no compatible crown, likely subcanopy.

Crown base is where the crown core's vertical profile breaks (a gap over 1.5 m). Values near zero mean branches or understory run continuously to the ground. Crown volume is the convex hull of crown returns above the base. Expect some height growth since 2022.

Imagery: RIDOT 3-inch leaf-off orthophotos from April 2022 and March 2026, served by RIGIS and draped on the lidar ground model. Orthophotos are rectified to the ground, so tall crowns lean a few metres away from the camera in the photo: ground features line up with the lidar, treetops may not.
